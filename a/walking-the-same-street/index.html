On walking the same street

There is a corner near my apartment I have passed perhaps a thousand times. A pharmacy on one side, a tiled wall on the other, a small tree that loses its leaves in October and remembers them again in March. Nothing about this corner is remarkable1, which is, of course, the point.

1Or rather: it is remarkable only to me, which is a different thing.

I used to think attention required novelty — a new city, a new room, a new view. But novelty makes a poor teacher2. It dazzles, and then it leaves. What I've come to trust instead is repetition. The same street, walked slowly, becomes a small instrument for noticing the way the light changes, the way the neighbours change, the way I change.

2Cf. Berger, Ways of Seeing — that a thing must be looked at twice before it begins to look back.

This morning the tiles were wet from a brief rain and the tree had begun to flower. I had not noticed, until today, that it flowered at all.
