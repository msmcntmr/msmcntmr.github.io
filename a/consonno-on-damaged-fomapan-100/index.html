Ruins of Consonno on damaged Fomapan 100

I visited the site of Consonno several times over the years. I believe the first time was in 2015 or thereabouts, and a second time a few years later; on both occasions I took digital photographs, first with a Pentax system and then with a Fujifilm one. A few years later, perhaps in 2019, I went back without any great expectations, carrying a 4×5-inch large-format camera. I say “without any great expectations” because I remember it being a swelteringly hot summer afternoon, I had some old Fomapan 100 film to use up, and I knew it was most likely damaged — so I could neither have nor wanted to travel somewhere farther away and more evocative, somewhere that would have deserved much better.

For those who may not know, Consonno is a municipality in the province of Lecco which, over a period of just under twenty years between 1960 and 1976, was chosen as the site for the construction of a “city of toys.” In the imagination of its creator, Mario Bagno, it was meant to become the ultimate entertainment centre: a place that was always open and always celebrating, as some of the signs along the road leading to the site once proclaimed.

For further information, here is the link to the relevant Wikipedia page.

In short, the whole thing eventually fell apart. First, a landslide blocked the village’s only access road. It was later repaired, but in the years that followed, fewer and fewer visitors apparently made their way there. The buildings slowly began to empty out and were abandoned, until a second landslide completely destroyed that same sole access road, effectively bringing Consonno itself to an end.

Many years later, this was compounded by the most mindless, Lord of the Flies-style mob mentality, which led to the defacement and destruction of the buildings and the site’s recreational and artistic works. Between rave parties and uncivil visitors, the area is now unrecognisable and quite dangerous to visit, with several structures in a state of collapse.

Returning to my damaged Fomapan 100 film, I knew that the drastic temperature changes it had undergone over the preceding years had almost certainly affected the emulsion. While loading the film holders, I noticed that the remaining sheets had stuck together. I no longer remember the exact number of sheets in that box, but I do know that only four from that batch survived — the ones shown here. The others were so badly damaged that they revealed none of what had been photographed.

Normally, I would have thrown them away without even exposing them, let alone developing them. But I think they are particularly fitting for what remains of Consonno.
